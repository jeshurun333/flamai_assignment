# Part C: Strategic Decision Memo — Conversational Tone Adaptation

**To:** Product & Engineering Leadership  
**From:** AI Team

**Subject:** Strategy Recommendation: Conversational Adaptation for Indic Languages  

---

### Executive Recommendation

We recommend **Path (c): Prompt-Engineering Only (System Persona & Few-Shot In-Context Framing)** as our launch path for the 3-week deadline, backed by a strict Day-4 Kill Criterion to pivot to Path (a) LoRA fine-tuning if necessary. 

Path (b) (<=1B inference rewriter) is immediately disqualified due to latency penalties, entity corruption risks, and double-serving overhead. Path (a) (full-model SFT) carries catastrophic unvetted risks across the 4 languages lacking reviewer coverage. Path (c) delivers immediate iteration, zero weight corruption, and maximum reviewer throughput under our strict constraints.

---

### 1. Explicit Assumptions

1. **Reviewer Capacity & Coverage Gap**: The single human reviewer works 10 hours/week and evaluates **only Hindi and Kannada**. Tamil, Telugu, Bengali, and Marathi (67% of target languages) have **zero dedicated native-speaker review capacity** before launch.
2. **Reviewer Evaluation Rate**: A thorough evaluation of a conversational response (assessing naturalness, register, tone, and semantic preservation against a formal reference) takes **2.5 minutes per response pair** ($\sim$24 pairs/hour).
3. **Hardware Availability**: 1× NVIDIA A100-80GB is available for exactly 14 days (336 GPU hours), after which training access terminates 7 days before launch review.
4. **Synthetic Data Constraint**: "No external API budget" dictates that any synthetic training data must be self-generated by local open weights on the single A100, which produces noisy colloquialisms and translation calques.
5. **Serving Budget**: The production serving stack must maintain existing p95 latency (<2.0s TTFT, <50ms ITL) without doubling GPU cluster capacity.

---

### 2. Back-of-the-Envelope Arithmetic

#### A. Reviewer Throughput Budget:
- **Weekly Capacity**: $10 \text{ hours/week} \times 24 \text{ evaluations/hour} = \mathbf{240 \text{ evaluations/week}}$.
- **Pre-Launch Total (Weeks 1 & 2)**: $240 \times 2 = \mathbf{480 \text{ total human evaluations}}$ ($\sim$240 for Hindi, $\sim$240 for Kannada).
- **Implication**: Any path requiring manual curation or validation of thousands of samples is mathematically impossible. Evaluation must focus on a gold benchmark of 200 test prompts per language.

#### B. Data Volume & GPU Compute Arithmetic:
- **Path (c) Prompt Engineering**: 
  - Token overhead: 1 persona system prompt ($\sim$60 tokens) + 2 conversational few-shot exemplars ($\sim$140 tokens) = **$\sim$200 prompt tokens**.
  - Serving cost impact: As demonstrated in Part B, adding 200 tokens of prefill increases TTFT by only $\sim$15–25 ms and consumes $\sim$22 KiB of KV-cache per sequence, with zero training compute required. The A100 is fully repurposed for automated validation sweeps.
- **Path (a) SFT on Synthetic Pairs**:
  - Minimum viable SFT dataset: 2,000 pairs/language $\times$ 6 languages = **12,000 pairs**.
  - Local synthetic generation on 1× A100: Generating 12,000 casualized pairs using local 4B/8B models at 40 tok/s takes $\sim$83 GPU hours ($\sim$3.5 days).
  - LoRA Fine-Tuning: 3 epochs on 12k pairs on A100 takes $\sim$18 GPU hours.
  - Total GPU time: $\sim$100 hours ($\sim$30% of total GPU budget), leaving zero room for failure or dataset regeneration.
- **Path (b) Inference Rewriter**:
  - Serving overhead: A secondary 1B model running autoregressively at inference adds $\sim$30–50 ms per generated token, **slashing generation goodput by 40%–50%** and doubling VRAM allocation per request.

---

### 3. Success Metric with Numeric Threshold

**Win Rate against Formal Baseline on Naturalness without Fact Regression**:
1. **Primary Metric (Reviewer Win Rate)**:
   In a blind side-by-side A/B test conducted by the native reviewer on 200 holdout conversational prompts in Hindi and Kannada:
   $$\mathbf{\ge 75\% \text{ Win/Tie Rate on Conversational Naturalness}}$$
   *(Target responses must be rated "natural casual colloquial" over "formal/textbook" while preserving polite respect markers).*
2. **Safety & Factuality Guardrail**:
   $$\mathbf{\le 2.0\% \text{ Semantic Distortion / Hallucination Rate}}$$
   *(Zero tolerance for slang introducing profanity, caste/gender bias, or altering numeric/factual details).*

---

### 4. Kill Criterion (Actionable & Time-Bound)

**Day 4 (Thursday of Week 1, 18:00):**
The reviewer will evaluate 50 prompt-engineered outputs each for Hindi and Kannada across 4 system prompt variants.

**The Kill Trigger**:
> If **no prompt-engineering configuration achieves $\ge 65\%$ win-rate over the formal baseline**, OR if prompt-engineering causes **$> 5\%$ instruction-following / language-drift failures** (e.g. reverting to English or hallucinating slang):
> **We immediately KILL Path (c) on Day 4 at 18:00.**

**Pivot Action**: We immediately execute the fallback plan on Day 5: train a parameter-efficient LoRA adapter (Path a) restricted **strictly to Hindi and Kannada**, preserving frozen base weights for the other 4 unreviewed languages.

---

### 5. Day 1 First Experiment

**Execution on Day 1 (Within 4 Hours of Approval):**
1. **Curate Benchmark**: Extract 50 representative user queries from real chat logs (greetings, advice, explanations, troubleshooting).
2. **Draft Prompt Variants**: Construct 4 distinct system prompt paradigms for Hindi and Kannada:
   - *Prompt 1 (Role-Play)*: Explicit persona conditioning (*"You are a friendly, witty native peer chatting on WhatsApp..."*).
   - *Prompt 2 (Register Constraints)*: Negative constraint rules (e.g., in Hindi: replace formal *"aap"* with polite-casual *"tum"*, ban Sanskritized vocabulary like *"kripya"*, mandate common colloquial markers like *"arre"*, *"haan bhai"*).
   - *Prompt 3 (2-Shot In-Context)*: 2 high-quality casual exemplar pairs translated by the reviewer.
   - *Prompt 4 (Hybrid)*: Role-play + 2-shot exemplars.
3. **Generate & Blind Review**: Run batch inference on the 50 queries across all 4 variants; deliver 50 randomized, blinded pairs to the reviewer on Day 2 morning for initial scoring.
